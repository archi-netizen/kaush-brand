The project

the WEEKEND: a reality TV show based on culinary drama.

A reality television studio proposed on the theatrical roofscape of the modern collection of the Städel Museum.

The aim was to use architecture together with a television reality show to extend, if not radically open, the institutional spaces of the Städel Museum and the Städelschule. The studio connects the campus physically and programmatically through a series of curated dining events, which are the show’s content.

The dining

the WEEKEND reconstructs “The Feast”, a dining event at the Rundgang 2016, and takes it to an architectural expression of the contemporary: social aesthetics, built from Reality TV entertainment and weekend brunches. The politics of a space and the social drama of its consumers give it a subjective experience that the project places above formal representation.

Concepts of spatial components and the idea of themes were brought together on one canvas and curated as a series of events. In the proposed setting they generated the content and footage for the show, and expressed a fusion of three borrowed elements.

1

Nostalgic social element · the social and theatrical space

The Feast

A dining performance held at the school’s annual exhibition: a multi-level dinner fused with a cuisine of eight core ingredients and a 50-minute musical. It gave the drama a multifaceted, event-based input. The thesis uses the social power of cooking and dining as the glue for the project’s reality.

2

Contemporary design tool · the core architectural space

The Collage

The thesis argued that contemporary architecture is not about design invention but a collage of elements borrowed from its surroundings to produce aesthetic effects. The project became a stage for the question “Architecture v/s the Image of Architecture”: the studio’s expression is a collage of photographs of its adjoining campus, recast in function and distorted and re-oriented until it contextualises itself.

3

Project medium · televisual space

The Weekend

Deeply influenced by Bernard Tschumi’s concept of “Space, Event & Movement”, the project needed architecture beyond space and form. Its façade is not only the physical reality of the collage but a curated, edited and post-produced version of how it needs to be captured. The series and its episodes are based on the drama of The Feast, staged twice a month at weekends.
