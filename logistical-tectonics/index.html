The Infrastructure Grid

An spatial investigation into India’s geographic central logistics capital.

This dossier reframes infrastructure not as passive transport channels, but as a rigid grid that dictates regional commerce, trade arbitrage, and physical distribution architecture.

By analyzing Nagpur’s Multi-modal International Cargo Hub (MIHAN), the Samruddhi Mahamarg, and regional freight corridors, this study maps the operational friction and structural thresholds of Central India’s supply chains. The core framework evaluates how networks handle localized regulatory, financial, and physical pressure thresholds.

The Five Spatial Typologies

The core framework establishes an “Infrastructure as Regional Grid” mapping methodology, defining five distinct facility typologies fitting Central India:

1
Rail-Linked / Multimodal. High-volume freight handling anchored by massive container rail sidings.
2
Grade-A Distribution. Master-planned 3PL and e-commerce spaces requiring 12m+ clear internal heights.
3
Automotive & Parts. Heavy floor-load spaces customized specifically for Just-In-Time (JIT) manufacturing cycles.
4
Cold Chain Infrastructure. Temperature-controlled zones vital for regional pharmaceutical and agricultural belts.
5
Bonded / FTWZ Warehousing. Duty-deferred customs-controlled territory directly leveraging airport proximity.

Feasibility & Threshold Metrics

To transition this spatial layout into an active performance model, each mapped node is benchmarked directly against structural limits, fiscal jurisdictions, and real-time transport network friction points:

  • Zoning & Tax Arbitrage: Spatial boundaries defining the stark operational divide between Central SEZ fiscal benefits (0% Custom/IGST rates) and standard domestic MIDC land frameworks.
  • Acreage Floor Minimums: Structural footprint boundaries mandating a minimum of 100 contiguous acres for rail-linked MMLPs and 25 acres for Grade-A multi-tenant hubs.
  • Logistical Friction Points: Tracking critical structural bottlenecks like the Waifad and Gumgaon toll plazas, which dictate peak-hour freight turnaround times (TAT).
  • Urban Access Windows: Modeling strict municipal daylight bans (08:00 to 22:00) for heavy utility vehicles attempting to enter inner city ring roads.
  • Grid Vulnerability: Mandatory dual-source infrastructure power backups required to shield temperature-sensitive pharma and horticulture assets from localized grid failures.
