How every brand project is built: the sequence, the questions and the discipline behind the decisions.

The methodology comes before the logo. Every project begins with the same sequence of questions, in the same order. Not as a rigid formula, but because the sequence follows the causal logic of how brands work.

Foundation first. Always.

The most expensive mistake in brand design is starting with the visual. A logo before a positioning statement is aesthetics without meaning. A palette before an audience is surface without structure.

These mistakes are recoverable, but they cost time, money and often the client relationship. So the work runs from the inside out, in six steps.

The sequence

Six steps, inside out.

01

Mission & Purpose

Why does this brand exist beyond revenue? What would be missing if it didn’t? This is not a tagline exercise. It is an honesty exercise.

02

Values

What does this brand believe non-negotiably? Not aspirationally, actually. The test: would they lose business to uphold it?

03

Positioning

Who is this brand for, and, crucially, who is it not for? Specificity is not a limitation. It is the mechanism of distinction.

04

Audience

Not a demographic but a psychographic. What do they fear? What do they value? Where do they look when they have the problem this brand solves?

05

Visual Language

Only now: colour, type, form, space. The visual system is the translation of the answers above into a language of perception.

06

Voice & Tone

How the brand sounds in every written context, from a pitch deck to an Instagram caption to an out-of-office reply.

The lens

The architectural lens in practice.

Architecture taught me that every design decision has a reason that comes before the aesthetics. Brand identity works the same way.

A typeface is not chosen because it looks nice. A colour is not pulled from a mood board. Each one earns its place.

The construction document principle

Brand guidelines are not a document for designers. They are for anyone who will ever apply the brand: contractors, printers, social media managers and future employees who never met the original team. They must be precise enough that a stranger can apply the brand correctly. That is the standard of a construction document.

What Vernacular Branding adds

Before any decision, ask about context.

The research behind Vernacular Branding, co-authored with Bhuleshwar Mate, asks a prior question: what is the visual language of the environment this brand will live in?

It separates brands that feel like they belong from brands that feel imported. These codes are not backgrounds. They are the operating context of every mark.

  • The grammar of the Indian bazaar
  • Typography in Frankfurt’s financial district
  • The visual culture of the EU startup scene
