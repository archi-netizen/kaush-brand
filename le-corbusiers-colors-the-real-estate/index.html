Le Corbusier’s Colors & Real Estate

Architectural polychromy as a tool for real estate, public buildings and campuses.

Colour as a systemFocus
1931 & 1959Palettes
Real estate & campusApplied to

Le Corbusier’s architectural polychromy gave colour a job: to order space, to show function and to make buildings easy to read. A standard palette can do the same for modernist buildings, public buildings, offices, housing and restorations.

Where it fits

Five kinds of building.

01

Modernist architecture

Clean lines and simple forms carry a colour system best. Large white planes with a few saturated walls.

02

Public buildings

Polychromy was made for museums, schools and offices: colour brings order and helps people find their way.

03

Commercial buildings

A standard palette gives shopping centres and offices a cohesive, professional face for customers and clients.

04

Residential buildings

In entrances and corridors, a consistent colour sequence creates continuity throughout the building.

05

Restoration

For historic buildings with a defined style, the system helps select colours and protect the original look.

A university renovation

Five ways the system helps.

01

A cohesive design

A campus is large and complex. One standard colour system unifies many buildings.

02

Function first

Polychromy was made to show a building’s function: a colour for each kind of space.

03

A visual guide

Colour tells everyone involved how the design works and keeps its elements together.

04

History, built in

The system was made for public buildings, and a university is one. Colour connects it to its past.

05

A welcoming place

Colour makes an atmosphere that improves well-being and productivity for students, staff and visitors.
